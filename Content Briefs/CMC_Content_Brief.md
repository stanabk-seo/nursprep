## 4. Search Intent and SERP Notes

The page must answer, in order:

1. *What is CMC and who issues it?* (most users land confused between CMC, CSC, and CV-BC)
2. *Am I eligible?* (the prerequisite-credential rule blocks many readers immediately)
3. *What is on the exam?* (blueprint — note that AACN does not publish percentage weights for CMC; show the published content categories instead and label them clearly)
4. *What does it cost?* (subspecialty price is materially lower than CCRN/PCCN)
5. *How do I renew?* (CMC renews with the underlying CCRN/PCCN — surprising for many)
6. *How do CMC, CSC, and CV-BC differ?*

Long-tail to cover: "cmc certification requirements", "cmc exam pass rate", "aacn cmc renewal", "cmc vs csc", "is cmc worth it", "cmc study plan".

### H1 — CMC Certification: AACN Cardiac Medicine Subspecialty Guide

Opening lede (40–60 words): answer-first. State that CMC is the AACN subspecialty layered on top of CCRN or PCCN for nurses caring for acutely ill adult cardiac patients (non-surgical scope). Mention 90 items, 2-hour exam, 3-year renewal. (see facts.exam_format, facts.question_count, facts.renewal)

### H2 — CMC certification at a glance (overview)

Two short paragraphs. Paragraph 1: define CMC, name AACN Certification Corporation, state subspecialty status. Paragraph 2: cardiac medicine scope — ACS, heart failure, dysrhythmias, hemodynamic monitoring — distinguish from CSC (post-cardiac-surgery scope). Stats strip with 4 cells: 90 items, 2 hrs, 3 yrs renewal, prereq required (CCRN/PCCN). (see facts.question_count, facts.time_limit_minutes, facts.renewal, facts.eligibility)

### H2 — Am I eligible for the CMC exam? (eligibility)

Answer-first: "Yes, if you already hold CCRN, PCCN, CCRN-E, or ACCNS-AG plus an active US RN/APRN license — and you can document cardiac patient-care hours." Then the two hour pathways verbatim from research.json. Add a "strong candidate" bullet list: hours route, unit type, comfort with telemetry/hemodynamics/vasoactives. (see facts.eligibility)

### H2 — CMC exam blueprint — content categories (blueprint)

Definition block: explain that AACN publishes a CMC test plan grouping items into content categories but does **not** publish percentage weights the way CCRN/PCCN do. List the published categories (ACS, Heart Failure & Cardiomyopathies, Dysrhythmias & Conduction, Hemodynamic Monitoring & Cardiogenic Shock, Vascular Disease & Pulmonary Hypertension). Tell candidates to verify weights against the current AACN CMC Exam Handbook. (see blueprint[*])

### H2 — Cost, scheduling, and membership savings (cost)

Rows table with member ($180) vs non-member ($265). Mention that subspecialty pricing is meaningfully lower than CCRN/PCCN because CMC builds on an existing credential. Renewal subheading: 3-year cycle synchronized with underlying CCRN/PCCN; 432 practice hours during cycle + Synergy CERPs OR re-exam. (see facts.fee, facts.renewal)

### H2 — How hard is the CMC exam? (passrate)

Be candid: AACN does not publish year-by-year first-time pass rates for the CMC subspecialty. Set expectations honestly — most candidates pass because they already hold CCRN/PCCN and self-select for cardiac practice — but do **not** invent a percentage. Recommend a structured 4–6 week plan and one full-length timed practice. (see facts.pass_rate_recent — null)

## 6. Voice and Tone Notes

- Answer-first: open every H2 with the direct answer in the first sentence.
- Repeat the noun: write "CMC" or "the CMC exam," not "it" or "this."
- Exact numerics: 90 items, 2 hours, 3 years, $180/$265, 432 practice hours.
- No invented percentages. Where AACN does not publish (pass rate, blueprint weights), name the gap and tell candidates how to verify against the current handbook.
- Address candidates as "you." Avoid filler ("dive into", "essential", "comprehensive").
