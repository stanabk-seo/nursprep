## 2. Critical Disambiguation (Read First)

**RNC-OB is awarded by the National Certification Corporation (NCC)** — an NCCA-accredited certifier headquartered in Chicago that issues the obstetric, gynecologic, and neonatal nursing core credentials. The exam is delivered through PSI/AMP, both at testing centers and via Live Remote Proctoring (LRP). (`facts.awarding_body`, `facts.exam_format` in `Content/RNC-OB_research.json`.)

Writer must spell out **Registered Nurse Certified - Inpatient Obstetric Nursing** on first mention, then use "RNC-OB" throughout. Never write "RNC-OB course" — RNC-OB is an exam-based board credential.

**Credentials readers confuse with RNC-OB:**

| Credential / Concept | Awarding body | Why it confuses people | How to handle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RNC-MNN** | NCC | Same prefix, same body, adjacent scope | One-line contrast: RNC-MNN (Maternal Newborn Nursing) targets Level I postpartum / mother-baby / well-newborn nursing. RNC-OB covers the full inpatient OB continuum including intrapartum and high-risk antepartum after 20 weeks. (`competing_credentials`) |
| **C-EFM** | NCC | Same body; many L&D RNs hold both | One-line contrast: C-EFM is a subspecialty add-on focused only on electronic fetal monitoring. It does not stand alone as a core credential. RNC-OB is the core. |
| **RNC-LRN** | NCC | Letter overlap; similar look | One-line contrast: RNC-LRN (Low-Risk Neonatal Nursing) is for well-newborn-nursery RNs. RNC-OB is mother-side, not infant-side after stabilization. |
| **OBE** (Obstetric Emergencies, new 2025) | NCC | New subspecialty in OB space | One-line note: OBE is a team-focused subspecialty exam launched in 2025 for OB emergency response. It is not a substitute for RNC-OB. |
| **WHNP-BC** | NCC / ANCC | Same body issues both | One-line contrast: WHNP-BC is the APRN credential for Women's Health Nurse Practitioners. Different role, different educational path. |
| **CNM** | AMCB | Sounds adjacent in OB | One-line contrast: CNM (Certified Nurse-Midwife) is an APRN-level midwifery credential from a separate certifier (AMCB), not NCC. |

### H2 — How Do I Apply for the RNC-OB Exam?

Numbered step-by-step:

1. Confirm 24-month / 2,000-hour eligibility against your work records (timesheets, badge access, employer letter).
2. Create an account at nccwebsite.org. Applications are online only — NCC does not accept mail, fax, or email.
3. Submit your application with the **$325** total fee ($50 non-refundable application + $275 testing). (`facts.fee_total`)
4. NCC reviews the application within **1 to 14 days**. (`facts.exam_format`)
5. Once approved, you receive an eligibility letter and a **90-day window** to schedule. (`facts.scheduling_window_days`)
6. Choose between PSI/AMP test center or **Live Remote Proctoring (LRP)** and schedule your seat. NCC strongly recommends scheduling within the first 30 days of the window — sites cannot be guaranteed later.
7. Test day: bring valid government-issued photo ID matching the application name. For LRP: complete the system check ahead of time and follow PSI's room-clearing protocol.
8. Score reporting: **no immediate result**. Pass/fail is posted to your NCC account within **15 business days**, with the score report 24 hours later.

- NCC publishes year-by-year RNC-OB (INPT) pass rates on its public statistics page. (`pass_rate_history`)
- **2025:** 2,079 candidates, **65% pass rate** — a sharp drop from 2024.
- **2024:** 3,098 candidates, **86% pass rate**.
- **2023:** 1,546 candidates, **90% pass rate**.
- **2022:** 2,424 candidates, **87% pass rate**.
- **2021:** 1,963 candidates, **93% pass rate**.
- **2020:** 2,257 candidates, **90% pass rate**.
- Writer guidance: be honest about the 2025 drop. The 21-point shift from 86% to 65% is unusual and is consistent with a new exam form or a revised passing point coming online. NCC has not publicly attributed the change. Frame as: *"Historically RNC-OB has run in the 86–93% pass range. - Note: NCC uses **item response theory (Rasch)** scoring with equating across forms, so there is no fixed percentage cutoff and no released raw score. Pass/fail is determined against a Content-Team-set ability criterion. (`facts.passing_score`)

### H2 — How Do I Renew RNC-OB Certification?

- RNC-OB is valid for **3 years**. (`facts.renewal_cycle_years`)
- Renewal runs through NCC's **Continuing Competency Assessment (CCA)** framework. At the start of each new 3-year cycle, the certificant takes the CCA, which generates a personalized **Education Plan** listing the specific CE hours needed. (`facts.renewal_process`)
- Only CE earned **after** completing the CCA and aligned to the resulting Education Plan counts toward maintenance.
- A maintenance application plus a maintenance fee must be submitted before the due date. NCC modules earned for the plan are pre-loaded for audit; outside CE certificates must be retained for the entire cycle in case of random audit.
- Lapsed credential: holders who let RNC-OB expire must reapply and sit the exam again — there is no extended grace period.
- Important difference from other certifiers: NCC does **not** publish a single fixed CE-hour requirement that applies to every RNC-OB holder. Hours are individualized based on CCA performance. Writer should explicitly call this out — many readers expect "X CE hours every Y years" and will be confused otherwise. (`discrepancies.renewal_ce_hours`)

### H2 — RNC-OB vs RNC-MNN, C-EFM, OBE: Which One Fits?

| Credential | For | Awarding body | One-line scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RNC-OB** | Inpatient OB RNs | NCC | Full inpatient OB continuum from 20 weeks through discharge — including L&D |
| **RNC-MNN** | Mother-baby / postpartum RNs | NCC | Level I postpartum and well-newborn care |
| **RNC-LRN** | Newborn nursery RNs | NCC | Low-risk neonatal nursing (well-newborn) |
| **C-EFM** | L&D RNs, providers, perinatal educators | NCC | Electronic fetal monitoring subspecialty — add-on, not standalone |
| **OBE** | OB emergency response teams | NCC | Obstetric emergencies subspecialty (launched 2025) |
| **WHNP-BC** | APRNs in women's health | NCC | Women's Health Nurse Practitioner — APRN role |
| **CNM** | APRN midwives | AMCB | Certified Nurse-Midwife — different certifier |

(`competing_credentials`)

**Take RNC-OB if most of your hours are in L&D, antepartum, or full-scope inpatient OB.** Move to RNC-MNN if your day is almost entirely postpartum / mother-baby. Stack C-EFM on top of RNC-OB if you spend significant time at the monitor strip — many L&D RNs hold both. OBE is for emergency-team focus.
