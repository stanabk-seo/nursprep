1. Always Answer the Main Question First
Every article exists to answer one main question.

Rule

    ●​ Answer it clearly in the first 1–2 sentences
    ●​ Use a simple statement

Format to follow

         X is Y.

Example

❌ Bad:
         Many people argue about the correct temperature for chicken…

✅ Good:
         The safe internal temperature for cooked chicken is 165°F (74°C).


2. Be Exact: Use Numbers, Units, and Facts
If a topic expects numbers, include them.

Common units Google expects

    ●​    Temperature → °C / °F
    ●​    Length → characters, pixels
    ●​    Time → minutes, days, weeks
    ●​    Quantity → percentage, grams, years

❌ Bad:
         Title tags should not be too long.

✅ Good:
         Google typically displays 50–60 characters in a title tag.
3. Keep Sentences Short and Direct
Complex sentences confuse Google.

Rules

    ●​ One sentence = one idea
    ●​ Avoid unnecessary phrases
    ●​ Avoid storytelling before the answer

❌ Bad:
        The most accurate way of determining whether the chicken has been cooked properly…

✅ Good:
        Cooked chicken is safe at 165°F.


4. Don’t Delay the Answer
Google looks for clear conclusions, not opinions or surveys.

Rule

    ●​ Answer first
    ●​ Explain later

❌ Bad opening:
        There are many opinions about how often hair should be washed…

✅ Good opening:
        Most people should wash their hair 2–3 times per week.




5. Avoid Confusing Pronouns (it, they, this)
Google may lose track of what “it” refers to.

Rule
    ●​ Repeat the noun if needed
    ●​ Clarity > style​


❌ Bad:
         Title tags are important. They should be short.

✅ Good:
         Title tags should be short.


6. Be Clear Even If the Topic Is Complex
If the real answer is “it depends,” still give a baseline answer first.

Pattern

         Clear answer → small clarification

✅ Example:
         Google usually shows 50–60 characters in a title tag, though this can vary by device.




7. Use Topic-Related Words Naturally
Google checks whether your content is truly about the topic.

Example: If writing about Title Tags, naturally include:

    ●​    SERP
    ●​    HTML
    ●​    Browser
    ●​    Meta tags
    ●​    Search results
    ●​    Click-through rate​


⚠️ These are supporting words, not keywords to force.
8. Cover the Reader’s Next Questions
Ask yourself:

         “What would a reader ask next?”

Example: “Emergency Fund”

You should cover:

    ●​    What it is
    ●​    How much to save
    ●​    Where to keep it
    ●​    How to build it

This makes the article feel complete to Google.




9. Make Names and Entities Clear
Entities = people, places, products, certifications, exams.

Rules

    ●​ Use full names when needed
    ●​ Add context words to avoid confusion

❌ Bad:
         Portland is expensive.

✅ Good:
         Portland, Oregon, is expensive.

Formatting tip

    ●​ Put important entities in headings, lists, or tables
    ●​ Don’t mix entity names with prices or years in the same heading​
10. Use Structure to Show Meaning
Google understands formatting.

Use:

   ●​   Headings (H2, H3)
   ●​   Bullet lists
   ●​   Numbered steps
   ●​   Tables for comparison
   ●​   Bold labels at paragraph start​



Structure logic

   ●​   Headings define the content below them
   ●​   Numbered headings = steps or ranking
   ●​   Lists = grouped items
   ●​   Tables = attributes or comparisons​



11. Write in Clear Content Blocks
Google reads content in layers:

   ●​   Sentence
   ●​   Paragraph
   ●​   Section
   ●​   Page

Each section should:

   ●​ Make sense alone
   ●​ Support the main topic
   ●​ Not contradict nearby sections
